# Claim boundary

## Supported claims

Under the frozen v0.3 protocol and within the tested causal microworld:

- component-aligned, designer-defined need changed allocation of scarce sensing;
- actual need increased damage-target sensing relative to total-need-preserving
  yoked need;
- actual need improved replicated restoration probabilistically;
- the evidence-trace difference persisted under a common decoder; and
- actual need passed the prespecified neutral-world false-repair safety gate.

The common-decoder result is a mediation diagnostic consistent with an
evidence-trace interpretation. It is not an estimate of a natural direct effect.

## Unsupported claims

The results do not demonstrate:

- constitutive autonomy;
- endogenous normativity;
- constitutive death;
- biological individuality;
- self-produced maintenance variables or viability conditions;
- general scientific autonomy;
- transfer to arbitrary environments; or
- human-level cognition.

## Functional model versus constitutive autonomy

The software implements a functional model of maintenance-coupled inquiry.
Need variables, normal ranges, damage generation, repair operators, costs,
intervention vocabulary, worlds, and stopping rules are supplied by the
designer. Successful regulation inside that design is not evidence that the
system constitutes or regenerates its own identity and norms.

## Designed need versus endogenous normativity

`actual_need` is aligned with a focal damaged component by the experimental
design. `yoked_need` preserves total need magnitude while disrupting that
alignment. Their contrast tests the functional consequence of alignment; it
does not show that the agent generated the relevance relation or its values
endogenously.

## Recovery versus causal correctness

An internal value can improve after an intervention even when the external
anomaly did not cause the damage. The benchmark therefore separates
organization restored, bridge decision correct, false repair, and replicated
restoration. The `no_null_no_validation` ablation illustrates the distinction:
high apparent recovery can coexist with causally incorrect repair.
